Executive summary
• The sleep pipeline is directionally strong (duration, continuity, and regularity as primary pillars), but
it underutilizes the newly available exact bedtime and wake-up timestamps; sleep timing regularity
should be upgraded from a single “midpoint deviation” feature to a multi-feature timing model using
validated constructs such as the Sleep Regularity Index (SRI) and social jetlag. 1
• The current sleep-need anchor (480 min) and lower clamp (420 min) are likely misaligned with a
student-heavy user base; consensus guidance indicates teens should sleep 8–10 hours and that <8
or >10 hours “on a regular basis” is inappropriate. 2
• Continuity thresholds can be made more evidence-aligned by explicitly anchoring efficiency and
WASO to consensus cutpoints (e.g., sleep efficiency ≥85% as a “good quality” indicator; WASO ≤20
min as “good quality,” and higher WASO ranges as “not good quality”). 3
• Sleep-stage penalties should be de-emphasized and capped because: (1) expert consensus is weaker
for architecture than for continuity variables, and (2) consumer wearables have limited stageclassification agreement with PSG, making stage deficits noisy as a student-facing “score driver.” 4
• Physical load modeling (TRIMP-like impulse + CTL/ATL with 42d/7d time constants) is broadly
acceptable and aligns with widely used impulse-response implementations; literature and training
platforms commonly cite 42 days (fitness) and 7 days (fatigue) as nominal parameters. 5
• Recovery inputs (log-HRV, RHR, RR) are directionally correct; RR is supported as a wearable-derived
physiological measure and has been used in illness/strain detection contexts, but you should
strengthen baselining, missingness handling, and “trend vs. snapshot” decomposition. 6
• Mental load modeling is the weakest subsystem scientifically (expected, given the field), but the
bigger engineering issue is stability: MAI-like ratios can explode when the chronic reference/
denominator is small and session coverage is sparse. This is solvable deterministically with floors,
shrinkage, calibration gating, and confidence-linked clamping.
• The “urgency-only impulse” fallback (600·urgency) is likely to create artifacts (high mental strain
without studying) and can amplify MAI instability; a better design is to separate “pressure” (urgency)
from “work done” (study impulse) and only allow urgency to modulate targets/states rather than
create large load by itself. 7
• Calibration should be formalized (physical and mental): many successful consumer systems delay or
down-weight training-status interpretations until sufficient history exists (e.g., Garmin training status
needing ~1–2 weeks, training-load focus needing ≥7 days and improving after ~4 weeks; Polar uses
7-day “strain” vs 28-day “tolerance”). 8
• The highest-impact near-term changes are: (1) replace sleep regularity with SRI + social jetlag + drift
features, (2) implement sparse-data stabilization for MAI and mental states (denominator floor +
shrinkage + gating), and (3) add explicit calibrationStatus/readiness objects and denominator
diagnostics to the API contract to prevent misinterpretation and debugging blind spots.

Calibration protocol
This protocol is decision-complete and designed to (a) prevent misleading states/targets early, (b) use 365day history immediately when present, and (c) scale cleanly to multi-year histories.

Definitions
• A qualifying cardio session is one that meets at least one evidence-aligned intensity criterion:
• HR-based: ≥20 min with mean HRR ≥0.40 (moderate intensity threshold). 30
• MET-based fallback: ≥30 min with estimated MET ≥3.

31

• A valid training day is a day with ≥1 qualifying cardio session.
• A valid impulse day is a day with any computed impulse ut > 0 (qualifying or not), but only
qualifying days count toward calibration status for training states/targets.

• phase ∈ {uninitialized, calibrating, calibrated, degraded}
• effectiveTrainingDays28 (count of valid training days in last 28)
• effectiveTrainingDays42 (count of valid training days in last 42)
• qualifyingSessionCount42
• lastQualifyingSessionDate
• reasonCodes[]

overtraining_risk) and show only {no_status} . - Targets shown as “baseline movement” suggestions, not
load targets. - Confidence for physical domain hard-capped: Cphys ≤ 0.40.
Justification: consumer ecosystems commonly require initial history before training status stabilizes.

Sparse-data robustness protocol
This is decision-complete and focuses on preventing MAI explosions and nonsensical mental states when
study history is sparse (including “1 day out of 10”).

Diagnosis: why MAI explodes under sparse study history
MAI is structurally a ratio of an acute–chronic difference divided by a chronic reference. Under sparse data: MCL remains small (long τ , lots of zeros), while MAL can jump after a single large day (short τ ).
- If MCL_ref is estimated from early/small history (or tied to MCL itself), it can become too small, making
division yield extreme values.
- An urgency-only impulse can create load on “no study” days, altering MAL/MCL dynamics and
compounding instability.
These are deterministic mathematical consequences of EWMA dynamics and ratio scaling, not “data
glitches.”

Safeguards (must all be implemented together)
Denominator floors (hard safety)
• Define:

MCL_ref∗ = max(MCL_ref, 100)
using your existing min_ref=200 ⇒ 0.5·min_ref floor = 100.
Also apply the same idea to physical AI:

CTL_ref∗ = max(CTL_ref, 0.5 ⋅ min_refu )
with min_refu =150 ⇒ floor 75 (heuristic mapping).

use cold_ref. - When valid, shrink to cold_ref with κ = 20 days as in the revised formulas.
This prevents “tiny denominators” from short history.
