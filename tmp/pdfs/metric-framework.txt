Metric Framework for Sleep, Recovery, Physical
Strain, and Mental Strain
Executive Summary
A) Executive Summary


This design specifies an explicit, deterministic, daily-recomputed metric system for a web + iOS product that
ingests Apple Health/Watch-derived physiology/activity data and study behavior logs. It produces WHOOP-
style core metrics (Sleep, Recovery, Physical Strain) and adds a new, mathematically parallel metric family
for cognition (Mental Recovery, Mental Strain) with optimal daily target ranges for both strain types.


Final recommended architecture

The framework is a two-domain impulse-and-recovery model:


     • Physical domain
     • Load input: heart-rate-derived training impulse (TRIMP-like) aggregated daily. 1
     • State variables: acute physical fatigue and chronic physical capacity, updated via explicit exponentially
       weighted moving averages (EWMAs) over 7d and 28d, with a 180d+ baseline for personalization and
       drift control. 2

     • Outputs: Physical Strain (0–21), Physical target range [low, high] (0–21), and contributions to
       Recovery.


     • Mental domain


     • Load input: study-session “cognitive TRIMP” (minutes × intensity multipliers) plus deadline/exam
       pressure terms (as a background stressor), all daily. (Intensity and pressure mappings are explicitly
       defined; pressure and circadian disruption are partly heuristic but grounded in stress and circadian-
       misalignment evidence.) 3
     • State variables: acute mental fatigue and chronic mental capacity, updated via the same multi-
       horizon mechanics.

     • Outputs: Mental Recovery (0–100), Mental Strain (0–21), Mental target range [low, high] (0–21).


     • Cross-domain recovery
       Recovery is computed primarily from sleep and autonomic physiology (HRV, resting HR, respiratory
       rate) interpreted relative to each user’s long-term baseline and recent load states. HRV is explicitly
       treated as a stress-sensitive autonomic marker and must be interpreted longitudinally. 4




                                                      1
     • Robustness + confidence
       Every metric is emitted with a confidence score that depends on: (a) signal availability, (b) sensor
       quality proxies and outlier checks, and (c) amount of historical context (up to 180d+). Sleep-stage
       components are down-weighted because sleep staging accuracy in consumer devices is imperfect
       and device-dependent. 5


Why this improves over typical simple readiness/load blends

    1. Explicit multi-horizon dynamics (7d, 28d, 180d+) are first-class rather than incidental EWMAs.
       Acute state and chronic capacity are separate, and long-term baselines are used for calibration, drift
       detection, and confidence management. 2
    2. Sleep is decomposed into duration, continuity/efficiency, regularity, and timing, aligned with
       established sleep science (including circadian/homeostatic concepts) and modern regularity metrics
       (SRI). 6
    3. Mental Strain is structurally parallel to Physical Strain (impulse accumulation + recovery
       dynamics), making it interpretable and implementable instead of being a vague “productivity score.”
    4. Targets are ranges, not points, and are computed from recovery + recent under/over-load, so they
       can expand or contract in a mathematically guaranteed way:
    5. Better recovery → higher optimal strain (both domains).
    6. High mental recovery + ~2 days of low study → target mental strain increases (explicitly encoded in
       the target equation).
    7. Heuristics are isolated (e.g., time-of-day multipliers, deadline-pressure mapping) and separable
       from evidence-backed foundations (TRIMP, sleep regularity, stress–HRV links).

D) Candidate Model Families


Family one: TRIMP-like daily load + multi-horizon EWMAs

Definition: compute daily load Ld (TRIMP for physical, “cognitive TRIMP” for mental), then compute: - acute
Ad = EW M A7d (L) - chronic Cd = EW M A28d (L) - long-term baseline Bd = EW M A180d (L) (or
robust median/MAD)


Pros: simple, stable, interpretable, deterministic; easy to implement daily; robust to missing days via EWMA
decay. 37
Cons: EWMAs approximate physiology; without richer modeling, it does not explicitly estimate fitness/
fatigue separately.
Failure modes: sparse HR data can undercount physical load; sparse study logging can undercount mental
load unless background pressure is modeled.


Family two: Fitness–fatigue impulse-response model (Banister/Morton style)

Definition: maintain two latent states from training impulse: fitness and fatigue, each with exponential
decay, and combine them to predict readiness/performance. 23


Pros: strong conceptual grounding; explicitly separates acute fatigue from slower adaptation.
Cons: parameter identification can be unstable; requires careful priors/guardrails and/or observed




                                                     7
performance markers; pitfalls and sensitivity to initialization are documented. 24
Failure modes: overfitting and non-identifiable parameters without additional outcome signals (e.g., actual
performance tests).


Family three: Latent recovery state-space model (Kalman / Bayesian DLM)

Definition: treat “recovery” as a latent state updated by noisy observed indicators (sleep features, HRV,
RHR, RR) with physically plausible dynamics.


Pros: naturally handles missing/noisy data and yields principled uncertainty (confidence intervals).
Cons: higher implementation complexity; requires careful tuning/priors; harder to explain to users
compared to deterministic weighted sums.


Family four: Cognitive load accumulation with time-of-day and deadline pressure

Definition: daily mental load from study sessions + “pressure background”; mental recovery depends on
sleep and autonomic markers; circadian misalignment modifies both. This directly matches evidence that
circadian disruption impairs cognition and that exam stress affects sleep and autonomic activity. 38


Pros: implementable from available data; interpretable and directly connected to user behavior; supports
explicit target-range rule for “under-studying + high recovery.”
Cons: intensity estimation is partially heuristic without subjective ratings; optional self-report (e.g., NASA-
TLX) can improve calibration. 30


Recommendation: Implement Family one + Family four as the production default (deterministic and
stable), and optionally add a Family three layer later for uncertainty modeling once historical datasets are
large and validated.

Multi-Horizon Trend Integration and Personalization
F) Multi-Horizon Trend Integration


This system explicitly uses three horizons, each with a distinct role:




                                                       17
Seven-day horizon

Role: acute fatigue and recent perturbation detection. - EW M A7d for physical (APL) and mental (AML)
load - also compute 7d variability of HRV (optional): weekly mean and CV are commonly used in athlete
monitoring discussions. 20


Operationally: - If AP Ld or AM Ld is high relative to baseline, Recovery and Mental Recovery are penalized
                         phys
via zAP L , zAM L . - If Rd     or Rdment rises above ~1.2, targets are automatically reduced (overreach penalty).


Twenty-eight-day horizon

Role: “current capacity” estimate and near-term adaptation. - EW M A28d for chronic physical load CP Ld
and chronic mental load CM Ld . - Targets are primarily anchored to these chronic values and then
modulated by same-day recovery.


One-hundred-eighty-day (or more) horizon

Role: personalization baseline, drift control, seasonality, calibration anchors. - Robust medians/MADs for
physiology (HRV, RHR, RR, sleep duration, sleep midpoint, stage index), and quantiles for strain and mental
load. - Helps distinguish “today is abnormal for you” from “you have adapted over months.” - Controls cap
parameters Capphys , Capment and underload thresholds.


When long-term baseline dominates vs acute dominates

      • Acute dominates when (a) z-scores are extreme (e.g., HRV drops far below baseline, RHR/RR jump),
        or (b) acute-to-chronic ratio indicates rapid ramp. This corresponds to day-level homeostatic
        perturbations. 47
      • Long-term dominates when acute signals are missing/noisy or low-confidence, and the user has
        stable long-term patterns. In that case, targets lean on CP Ld , CM Ld and long-term baselines, with
        wider ranges.

Adaptation logic

Adaptation is treated as gradual changes in chronic loads and baselines: - If CP Ld steadily increases over
weeks with stable recovery, future target ranges rise because “capacity” has grown. - If recovery is
chronically low while CP Ld stays high, ranges narrow and shift downward until recovery improves.


This avoids overclaiming causal “fitness gain” without performance tests, and avoids the known
interpretational pitfalls of fully fitted fitness–fatigue models. 24

Missing Data and Data Quality
H) Missing Data + Data Quality


Fallback hierarchy by metric

Sleep score fallbacks

     • If TSTd missing but T IBd present: approximate TSTd = 0.85 T IBd (Heuristic) and reduce
       confidence sharply.
     • If stages (Deep/REM/Core) missing: skip Sstage (set to neutral 50) and reduce sleep confidence; this is
       justified because consumer stage accuracy varies and should not dominate. 48
     • If timestamps missing (cannot compute SRI): approximate regularity with 7d SD of sleep midpoint
       and bedtime (if available) or with variability of TST. Use Fischer-style regularity metrics as inspiration
       (but label as heuristic approximation if not true SRI). 49

Recovery score fallbacks

If HRV missing (common): - Use:


                  rd′ = −0.70zRHR,d − 0.50zRR,d + 0.60zSleep,d − 0.50zAP L,d − 0.40zAM L,d

and then Recd = 100σ(rd′ ), with reduced confidence.


If RR missing: - Drop the RR term only, recompute, reduce confidence.


Physical strain fallbacks

     • If workout time series HR missing but average HR is present: compute session TRIMP from average
       HR (still valid for TRIMP form). 1
     • If average HR missing but active energy and duration exist: estimate intensity from energy rate. This
       is Heuristic and should be bounded to low/moderate zones.
     • If no workouts logged:
     • Set P Lrawd = ksteps stepsd + kae activeEnergyKJd with small constants and low
       confidence, to capture low-intensity daily activity.

Mental strain fallbacks

     • If the user does not log study sessions reliably:
     • Set session sum to 0 but keep background MBackd from pressure.
     • Encourage optional “study timer” integration to reduce missingness.
     • If exam schedule/urgency is absent:
     • Set Pd = 0 (no pressure) and compute mental strain only from study time.

Outlier handling and sanity checks

Apply pre-scoring validation: - Future-dated samples: discard and flag data quality issue. - Impossible
physiological values: - RHRd < 25 or > 140: mark invalid. - RRd < 6 or > 40: mark invalid. - HRVd ≤ 0
or > 500 ms for SDNN: mark suspicious (not impossible but high); winsorize. - Sleep sanity: - TSTd > 16h:

I) Validation Plan


Offline backtesting protocol

Using de-identified historical users with ≥180 days:


    1. Recompute all metrics day-by-day as if in production (no peeking into future).
    2. Evaluate internal consistency and predictive validity against proxy outcomes:
    3. next-day autonomic markers (LHRV , RHR, RR) residuals
    4. next-day subjective fatigue if available (optional future)
    5. adherence metrics: whether target-range guidance increases consistent load without increasing low-
       recovery days

Scenario tests (must-pass)

     • Recovery monotonicity: hold sleep and autonomic inputs constant; increase APL and AML →
       Recovery must decrease.
     • Target rule enforcement:
     • Set MRecd high (e.g., 80–90), set last 2 days mental strain very low → mental target center and high
       bound must increase vs identical case without underload.
     • Sleep staging missingness: remove stage data; SleepScore should remain stable within a bounded
       deviation and confidence should drop appropriately. 48
     • Exam pressure: during known exam windows, pressure term should increase mental load and
       reduce mental recovery even at constant sleep, within bounded limits consistent with known exam-
       stress effects. 50

Acceptance thresholds (practical)

     • Median absolute day-to-day jitter (when inputs stable):
     • Sleep: <8 points
     • Recovery: <10 points
     • Strain: <1.5 units
     • Correlation targets (proxy validation):
     • SleepScore vs next-day Recovery: positive correlation at user level (not necessarily large, but
       consistent).
     • Acute load vs next-day Recovery: negative correlation.

Drift monitoring in production

     • Track distribution drift of key baselines (RHR, RR, HRV) and strain caps.
     • Detect device-level shifts (OS updates, sensor changes) by abrupt baseline jumps; confidence should
       drop until recalibrated.
